How Often Should You Do Pilates? A Weekly Guide by Your Goal

How often should you do Pilates? For most people, two to three times a week. Here is how to set your frequency by goal, pair it with other training, and rest.

By Carey Caratini, founder of Space Pilates + Wellness Society. Pilates instructor, NASM certified personal trainer and licensed mental health therapist.

For most people, two to three Pilates classes a week is a strong, sustainable rhythm: enough to build strength and control without crowding out rest or other training. Once a week still has real value, especially when you are starting out or using Pilates alongside other workouts. Because mat Pilates is low impact, many people can practice four or more days a week once their body is used to it.

Key takeaways

  • Two to three sessions a week suits most goals and most schedules.
  • Once a week is a worthwhile starting point and a good way to maintain what you have built.
  • Pilates is low impact, so frequent practice is realistic, but your body still needs recovery between harder days.
  • Consistency over months matters more than any single week.
  • Pair Pilates with strength, cardio or yoga to round out your week rather than replacing them.

How often to do Pilates, by goal

There is no single correct number. The right frequency depends on what you want from your practice and what else is in your week.

If you are brand new

Start with one or two classes a week. That gives you time to learn the fundamentals and notice how your body responds. Many beginners feel muscles they did not know they had after the first few sessions, so some space between classes helps.

After two or three weeks, if you are recovering well and enjoying it, add a session. Our guide to Pilates for beginners covers what to expect in those first classes.

If you want core strength and control

Two to three times a week is a good target. Strength and coordination build through repetition, and the cues you learn in one class stick better when the next one is only a few days away.

If you want better posture and mobility

Two to three classes a week, plus a few minutes of simple cues on the days in between, works well. Posture is shaped by what you do all day, so small daily habits add up. See Pilates for posture for at-home cues.

If Pilates is your main form of exercise

Three to four sessions a week, mixed with walking or another activity you enjoy, gives you a well-rounded routine. Pilates covers strength, mobility, balance and breath, but most people also benefit from some cardiovascular activity and some heavier strength work.

If you are using Pilates to support another sport

One to two sessions a week can complement running, lifting, cycling, golf or tennis. Pilates fills in the trunk control, hip stability and mobility those sports rely on.

Combining Pilates with other training

Pilates plays well with almost everything. A few practical pairings:

  • Pilates and strength training. Pilates builds control and deep core stability, while heavier lifting builds load capacity. Many people alternate days, or do Pilates the day after a hard lifting session as active recovery. More on this in Pilates and strength training.
  • Pilates and cardio. Running, cycling or brisk walking on non-Pilates days balances your week.
  • Pilates and yoga. Restorative yoga is a natural partner for rest days. It focuses on mobility, breath and release, which complements the active control of Pilates.

Rest and recovery

Low impact does not mean no recovery needed. Your muscles adapt between sessions, not during them. If you feel persistently sore, unusually tired, or notice your form slipping, take a rest day or choose a gentler class.

Some practical guidelines:

  • After a demanding class, give those muscles a day before working them hard again.
  • Gentle movement on rest days, like walking or restorative yoga, often feels better than doing nothing.
  • Sleep, hydration and food all affect how you recover.
  • If something hurts in a sharp or lingering way, stop and check with your doctor or physical therapist.

If you have an injury, are pregnant or postpartum, or have any medical condition, talk to your provider about how much activity is right for you, and tell your instructor so they can offer modifications.

Building a weekly routine

The best routine is the one you can keep. Start with a schedule that fits your real week, not your ideal one, and build from there.

Questions

Is it OK to do Pilates every day?

Many people can practice mat Pilates daily because it is low impact, especially if they vary the intensity. Listen to your body and build up gradually, and keep at least one easier day in your week.

Is once a week of Pilates enough?

Once a week still has value, especially when you are starting out or pairing Pilates with other training. For more noticeable changes in strength and control, two to three sessions a week works better for most people.

Should I do Pilates before or after strength training?

Either can work. Some people like a short Pilates-style warm-up before lifting to wake up the core, while others do full Pilates classes on separate days. Avoid a very demanding core session right before heavy lifts.

How long should a Pilates session be?

Most classes run 45 to 60 minutes, which is enough time to warm up, work through the full body, and settle at the end. Shorter home sessions can be useful between classes.
