About

The other
kind of room.

A studio built around one idea: that movement should give you room, not take more from you.

The Idea
SPACE is the pause between everything else.

In Pilates, finding space means lengthening the spine, decompressing the joints, and finding mental clarity. It is where you return to your body, your breath, and your awareness — free from pressure, noise, and performance.

Most fitness brands sell an outcome. This one sells a state — and the state is one people in Sanford are actually short of. Movement here is not about changing yourself. It is about coming back into alignment with yourself.

Fifteen people in a room. Three formats. No mirrors to perform for, no leaderboard, no one counting. Come back to your body — and leave with a little more room than you arrived with.

Carey
Pilates Instructor & Mental Health Counselor

Certified Pilates instructor and licensed mental health counselor with a master's from UCF. She teaches movement the way she was trained to understand the nervous system — as something that can be settled, not just worked.

The nervous-system framing in this studio is not marketing language. It is her clinical training. When Carey cues you to decompress your spine, she means it anatomically — and she understands what happens in the brain when a body finally gets to exhale.

Adam
Strength Coach  ·  14 Years

Fourteen years as a strength coach. Two gyms built and run. He programs the strength side of SPACE, and he does it the way strength should be programmed: slowly, deliberately, and with a reason for every set.

Strength is not the opposite of ease. It is what makes ease possible. A body that can carry more moves through the world with less strain. That framing is true to fourteen years of coaching — and it is what makes FORM different from anything else on the schedule.

Why We Built This
We wanted to build the other kind of room.

Between us, we have spent a long time watching people push themselves in rooms that never gave anything back. Rooms that sold urgency and manufactured deficit. Rooms that made you feel like you were always behind. We wanted to build the other kind of room. One where coming in is enough. One that gives back more than it takes.
