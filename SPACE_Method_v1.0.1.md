**SECTION 01**

**OUR PHILOSOPHY**

*Why We Exist*

> *SPACE is the pause between everything else.*

In Pilates, finding space means lengthening the spine, decompressing the joints, and finding mental clarity. It is where a member returns to their body, their breath, and their awareness --- free from pressure, noise, and performance.

Movement here is not about changing yourself. It is about coming back into alignment with yourself.

That idea is the foundation of everything in this manual. Every standard, every blueprint, and every format exists to protect it.

**Our Mission**

To build strength, control, and mobility through unhurried, breath-led programming --- and to give members back more room than they walked in with.

We coach the center first, control second, and consistency above everything else.

**Our Vision**

To create a training system that develops complete movers.

Not specialists in a single format. Not people who can only move well on the board or only on a mat.

Members who are strong, controlled, and at ease in their bodies regardless of where --- or how --- they are moving.

**Who Built This**

Carey is a certified Pilates instructor and a licensed mental health counselor with a master\'s from UCF. The way she teaches comes from a real, clinical understanding of the nervous system --- not just how a body looks in a mirror.

Adam has spent fourteen years as a strength coach and has built and run two gyms. He programs the strength side the way strength should be programmed: slowly, deliberately, and with a reason for every set.

Between them, they spent a long time watching people push themselves in rooms that never gave anything back. This manual is the standard for the other kind of room.

**What We Believe**

**Every Movement Has a Reason**

Speed is not the goal. Fatigue is not the goal.

Every exercise, every rep, and every class must have a purpose.

If a movement cannot clearly answer the question, \"What is this building?\" then it should not be programmed.

**Consistency Beats Intensity**

One great class changes very little.

Hundreds of well-designed classes performed consistently change everything.

The best class is not the one that leaves members depleted. It is the one they can recover from, return to, and improve upon for years.

**Capacity, Not Performance**

Strength is not the opposite of ease. It is what makes ease possible.

Regardless of age, experience, or goal, every member benefits from building greater capacity --- the strength, control, and range that make daily life take less out of them.

This is not about becoming an athlete. It is about a body that carries a member through their own life with less strain, for longer.

**The Center Is Foundational**

A strong, connected center makes everything else better.

It improves posture, protects the spine, reduces injury risk, and increases confidence in every other movement a member makes.

Every SPACE class is built on a foundation of center-first strength development.

**Movement Quality Comes Before Movement Quantity**

Perfect form is not required on day one. Intentional form is.

Members should own the pattern before chasing more resistance, more reps, or more speed.

Quality movement creates long-term progress. Poor movement creates limitations.

**Progression Creates Results**

Results come from progressive, intelligent challenge applied over time.

Random classes create random outcomes.

Every block of programming should build on the one before it, with clear intent and appropriate progression.

**Variety Without Randomness**

Members thrive on variety. Progress thrives on consistency. SPACE programming balances both.

Movement patterns stay consistent while exercises, tempo, resistance, and sequencing evolve to keep members engaged without sacrificing progress.

**Longevity Is the Goal**

Training should improve life, not interfere with it.

Members should leave the studio feeling longer and stronger --- not sore in ways that keep them from their next workout, their next round of golf, or their next pickup soccer game.

The objective is to build people who can keep moving well for decades.

**The SPACE Standard**

Every class should accomplish three things:

-   Build the member.

-   Teach the member.

-   Prepare the member.

If a class fails to accomplish one of those objectives, it should be reconsidered.

**What Defines a SPACE Class**

Every SPACE class should feel:

-   Purposeful.

-   Controlled.

-   Challenging.

-   Connected.

-   Progressive.

-   Repeatable.

-   Energizing.

Members should finish every session believing they moved a little better than when they walked in.

That is the standard. That is SPACE.

**SECTION 02**

**THE SPACE FOUNDATION PYRAMID**

*Building Complete Movers*

Every training system is built on a philosophy.

Some prioritize intensity. Some prioritize aesthetics. Some prioritize speed.

At SPACE, we believe exceptional results are built by developing foundational qualities in the correct order.

The SPACE Foundation Pyramid is the blueprint for every class we build and every member we coach.

Whether someone is recovering from injury, training for a race, chasing better posture, or simply wants to feel strong in their own body, the same principles apply.

A stronger foundation creates greater long-term results.

**The SPACE Foundation Pyramid**

VITALITY --- Confidence \| Longevity \| Everyday Carryover

FLOW --- Coordination \| Rhythm \| Full-Body Connection

STRENGTH --- Muscular Endurance \| Progressive Resistance \| Stability

CONTROL --- Precision \| Alignment \| Intentional Movement

BREATH & CENTER --- Breath Mechanics \| Powerhouse Activation \| Postural Awareness

Each level of the pyramid supports the level above it.

Without breath and center, control is limited. Without control, strength cannot be expressed safely. Without strength, flow becomes sloppy. Without flow, true vitality is never realized.

Programming should always strengthen the foundation before expecting members to perform at higher levels.

**Level One**

**Breath & Center**

Breath and center are the foundation of everything we do.

Before members chase more resistance, faster tempo, or advanced sequencing, they must first find their center and connect it to their breath.

Breath & Center includes:

-   Diaphragmatic and lateral breathing

-   Powerhouse (deep core, glutes, inner thigh) activation

-   Pelvic and rib cage alignment

-   Postural awareness

-   Neutral spine control

Perfect breath is not required on day one. Intentional breath is.

At SPACE, members earn intensity through breath and center connection.

**Level Two**

**Control**

Control is the foundation of safe, effective movement.

Regardless of a member\'s goal, better control improves nearly every physical quality.

Greater control allows members to:

-   Move with precision instead of momentum

-   Protect the spine and joints under load

-   Isolate the intended muscle group

-   Progress into more advanced work safely

Control is not limited to slow movement. It includes:

-   Eccentric control

-   Isometric holds

-   Unilateral stability

-   Rotational control

-   Tempo control

Every SPACE class develops control according to the member\'s specific level while maintaining long-term progression.

**Level Three**

**Strength**

Strength is the ability to produce and sustain force through a controlled range of motion.

It is much more than fatiguing a muscle.

SPACE programming develops:

-   Muscular endurance

-   Progressive resistance strength

-   Structural balance

-   Postural strength

-   Lean, functional muscle

Different formats emphasize strength differently, but every member benefits from progressively greater, better-controlled resistance.

**Level Four**

**Flow**

Flow is where breath, control, and strength begin working together.

Members in flow are not simply strong. They move efficiently. They transition smoothly. They connect one movement to the next without losing center.

Flow includes:

-   Coordination

-   Rhythm

-   Fluid transitions

-   Full-body integration

-   Sustained work capacity

These qualities allow members to express the strength and control they have built. Regardless of age or experience, every member can become more fluid.

**Level Five**

**Vitality**

Vitality is the outcome of the entire system.

The purpose of training at SPACE is not simply to complete a class or perform for a mirror.

The purpose is to become more vital --- more capable in the body a member actually lives in.

More vital members move better. They recover better. They carry themselves with confidence. They are better prepared for the demands of work, family, sport, and everyday life.

Vitality is the ultimate expression of what SPACE builds.

**Applying the Pyramid**

Every class should intentionally develop at least one level of the pyramid while supporting the levels beneath it.

A slow, precision-focused GROUND class primarily develops control. It also reinforces breath and center.

A GLIDE class primarily develops flow and strength endurance. It still requires control and center connection throughout.

A FORM class develops strength, but only because control and center already exist beneath it.

Programming should never skip the foundation in pursuit of a harder class.

The pyramid is not a checklist. It is a progression.

**Key Takeaways**

-   Breath and center create the foundation.

-   Control supports everything above it.

-   Strength allows control to be expressed under load.

-   Flow connects strength into full-body movement.

-   Vitality is the ultimate goal.

Every SPACE class should strengthen one or more levels of the pyramid while reinforcing the foundation beneath it.

This is how we build members who are not only stronger, but more capable for life.

**SECTION 03**

**THE SPACE PROGRAMMING STANDARDS**

*How We Design Every Class*

Programming is not the art of packing a class with as many exercises as possible.

It is the discipline of creating consistent, cumulative progress through breath-led, intentional training.

At SPACE, every class is built upon ten programming standards. These standards create consistency across every instructor, every format, and every member\'s experience.

Regardless of format, every class should reflect these principles. These standards are not suggestions. They are the expectations for every SPACE class.

**1. Breath Initiates Movement**

Every exercise should be cued from the breath outward, not the limb inward. Exhale on exertion. Inhale to prepare and lengthen.

**2. Center Before Limb**

The powerhouse activates before the arm reaches or the leg extends. If center is lost, the exercise should be regressed --- not powered through.

**3. Control Before Load**

A member earns added resistance, range, or tempo only after demonstrating control at the current level.

**4. Quality Repetitions Over Quantity**

Eight controlled, connected repetitions build more than twenty rushed ones. Volume should never come at the expense of form.

**5. Every Exercise Has a Named Purpose**

An instructor should be able to say, in one sentence, what a given exercise is building. If they can\'t, it should not be in the class.

**6. Progress the Pattern, Not Just the Difficulty**

Members should feel the through-line between a beginner variation and its advanced expression. Progression should never feel like a completely different exercise.

**7. Balance the Body, Not Just the Class**

Every class should balance flexion with extension, pushing with pulling, and left side with right side across the full sequence.

**8. Modify Without Diminishing**

A modification is a different path to the same objective --- never a lesser version of the class. Every member should leave feeling they got a complete workout.

**9. Coach the Room, Not Just the Reps**

Cueing should address alignment, breath, and intention throughout the set --- not just count repetitions.

**10. Recovery Is Programmed, Not Improvised**

Every class includes deliberate release and lengthening work. It is not something that only happens if time allows.

**Applying the Standards**

Before finalizing any class, an instructor should be able to answer yes to each standard.

If the answer is no to any standard, the class should be revised before it is taught.

**Key Takeaways**

-   Breath and center come before load, every time.

-   Purpose justifies every exercise on the sequence.

-   Balance and modification protect long-term member results.

-   Recovery is not optional --- it is programmed.

**SECTION 04**

**THE SPACE CLASS BLUEPRINT**

*Designing Every Class With Intention*

A great class is more than a sequence of exercises.

It is a progression of training pieces that prepare the member, develop a specific adaptation, reinforce quality movement, and leave them ready for their next session.

Every SPACE class follows a consistent blueprint. The emphasis may shift between formats, but the structure remains intentional.

**Step 1 --- Center & Breathe**

Before a single repetition, members find neutral alignment and connect to breath. This is not a throwaway minute. It sets the standard for the entire class.

↓

**Step 2 --- Warm & Connect**

The warm-up increases circulation, mobilizes the spine, and activates the powerhouse. Every warm-up should rehearse today\'s dominant movement pattern in a lighter form.

↓

**Step 3 --- Foundational Sequence**

Core-first work --- the exercises that reinforce center, alignment, and control before the class asks for more. This section teaches; it does not just tire.

↓

**Step 4 --- Format-Specific Development**

The primary work of the class --- the format\'s signature strength, resistance, or flow sequence. Members should begin this section prepared, not already fatigued.

↓

**Step 5 --- Supporting Work**

Targeted work that reinforces areas the primary sequence did not fully address --- often the opposing muscle group, an underused pattern, or unilateral balance.

↓

**Step 6 --- Full Expression**

Where the class comes together --- the most dynamic or challenging combination of the session, expressing the strength, control, and flow built throughout.

↓

**Step 7 --- Release & Lengthen**

Every class ends with deliberate stretching, myofascial release, or breathwork. Members should leave feeling longer, not just tired.

**Class Length**

Every class should respect the member\'s time. General guidelines:

  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------
  **GROUND**           45--50 Minutes

  **GLIDE**            45--55 Minutes

  **FORM**             45--50 Minutes
  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------

Longer classes should always provide greater value --- not simply more exercises.

**Class Flow**

Every class should follow a logical progression:

Center → Warm → Foundation → Develop → Support → Express → Release

A member should never wonder why they are performing a particular exercise. The flow should feel natural from beginning to end.

**Coaching Through the Class**

Every section of the class has a coaching emphasis.

  --------------------------------- --------------------------------------------------
  **Center & Breathe**              Teach breath and alignment.

  **Foundational Sequence**         Demand precision and connection.

  **Format-Specific Development**   Coach focus and execution.

  **Supporting Work**               Reinforce quality under fatigue.

  **Full Expression**               Coach pacing and full-body integration.

  **Release**                       Promote long-term recovery.
  --------------------------------- --------------------------------------------------

The instructor\'s role changes throughout the class. The member\'s experience should feel guided from beginning to end.

**Questions Every Instructor Should Ask**

Before finalizing a class, ask:

-   Does every section support today\'s objective?

-   Is the member centered and connected before load increases?

-   Does the sequence progress logically through the blueprint?

-   Is there unnecessary repetition without added value?

-   Will this class fit within the intended length?

-   Will members leave feeling longer, stronger, and successful?

If the answer to any question is \"no,\" revise the class.

**The SPACE Class Standard**

Every class should prepare the member.

Every class should improve the member.

Every class should respect the member\'s body and time.

When these three standards are consistently met, results follow naturally. This is how SPACE builds members for life.

**SECTION 05**

**BUILDING THE CLASS WEEK**

*Great Weeks Create Great Members*

Members do not change from a single class.

They change from weeks of purposeful, well-sequenced training, returned to consistently.

The class week is the foundation of the SPACE studio schedule. Each format should have a purpose within the week. Each class should prepare the member for what comes next.

When viewed individually, classes should be effective. When viewed together across the week, they should build a complete mover.

**The Weekly Philosophy**

Every studio week should answer three questions:

What are we helping members build this week?

How does today\'s class affect tomorrow\'s recovery and readiness?

Can a member attend consistently and still recover well enough to keep improving?

**A Sample Weekly Rhythm**

Most SPACE schedules balance the three formats to give members variety without sacrificing consistency:

  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------
  **Monday**           GROUND --- Foundational Strength & Control

  **Tuesday**          GLIDE --- Full-Body Flow

  **Wednesday**        FORM --- Strength & Resistance

  **Thursday**         GROUND --- Core & Mobility Focus

  **Friday**           GLIDE --- Full Flow

  **Saturday**         FORM --- Full-Body Strength

  **Sunday**           Rest / Recovery / Optional Restorative Class
  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------

This rhythm gives members a reason to return several times a week without repeating the same class twice. No class is positioned as the hard one or the easy one --- each simply has its own purpose.

**Every Class Needs an Identity**

Members should never feel like they took the same class twice in one week. Each class should have a unique focus even within the same format. Examples include:

  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------
  **Center**           Core and postural control focus

  **Build**            Progressive resistance and strength

  **Flow**             Coordination and full-body connection

  **Strength**         Targeted resistance and muscular endurance

  **Restore**          Mobility, release, and recovery
  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------

The goal is for members to recognize the purpose of a class before they read the description.

**Balance the Movement Patterns**

Every week should include balanced exposure to fundamental movement patterns:

  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------
  **Spinal**           Flexion, Extension, Rotation, Lateral Flexion

  **Lower Body**       Squat, Hinge, Lunge, Single-Leg

  **Upper Body**       Push, Pull, Stabilization

  **Core**             Anti-Extension, Anti-Rotation, Rotation, Flexion
  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------

The objective is balance across the week --- not necessarily within every single class.

**Rotate Movement Variations**

Members improve through repeated movement patterns. They stay engaged through exercise variation.

Example: the hundred progresses week over week --- from a supported, bent-knee version, to full extension, to an added resistance or tempo variation.

The pattern remains. The stimulus changes. This principle applies across every signature SPACE movement.

**Give the Week a Center of Gravity**

Every SPACE week should have a class members are quietly looking forward to --- not because it\'s harder than the rest, but because it\'s where everything they\'ve built that week comes together.

There is no leaderboard and nothing to prove. The class should simply feel like the fullest expression of the week --- a natural place to land, not a test to pass.

**Recovery Is Part of the Week**

Rest and restorative sessions are not missed opportunities. They are essential components of adaptation.

Recovery may include gentle mobility work, breathwork, or simply a day away from the studio. Members improve because they recover --- not because they never stop.

**The Rule of Three**

Every programming decision should satisfy three levels of purpose.

**Level One**

Every class should make sense on its own. An instructor should be able to explain the objective in one sentence. Every exercise should contribute to it.

**Level Two**

Every class should make even more sense within the week. No class exists in isolation --- each session should prepare members for what comes next while supporting what came before.

**Level Three**

Every week should make sense within the broader training block, progressing members toward a clearly defined outcome --- whether that\'s a stronger core, better posture, or simply a habit that sticks.

**SECTION 06**

**EXERCISE SELECTION & MOVEMENT ROTATION**

*Choosing What Belongs in the Sequence*

Not every exercise deserves a place in a SPACE class.

Exercise selection is the process of choosing movements that serve the class objective, respect the member\'s current level, and fit logically within the blueprint.

**The Movement Families**

Every SPACE sequence draws from five movement families, rotated deliberately across the week:

-   Flexion --- spinal curl, roll-up patterns, abdominal series

-   Extension --- back extension, swan, prone strengthening

-   Rotation --- obliques, spinal twist, cross-body integration

-   Lateral --- side-lying series, lateral flexion, hip stability

-   Mobilization --- spinal articulation, hip and shoulder mobility

A well-built class does not need every family in every session --- but a well-built week should touch all five.

**Selecting Within a Family**

Within each movement family, exercises are organized by demand --- not difficulty for its own sake. An instructor should be able to place any exercise on a simple continuum:

Supported → Assisted → Full Range → Loaded → Dynamic

Members move along this continuum as control improves --- not as a reward for attendance, but because their body has demonstrated readiness.

**The Regression-Progression Pair**

Every signature SPACE exercise should have a known regression and a known progression on hand at all times.

Regression is not a consolation prize. It is the correct choice when center or control is compromised. Progression is not automatic. It is earned.

**Rotating Exercises Without Losing the Pattern**

Variety keeps members engaged. Consistency builds skill. SPACE balances both by rotating the exercise while preserving the pattern.

  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------
  **Week One**         Roll-Up, supported with strap

  **Week Two**         Roll-Up, full range

  **Week Three**       Roll-Up with rotation

  **Week Four**        Roll-Up with added resistance
  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------

The pattern --- spinal flexion and articulation --- remains constant. The stimulus evolves.

**Common Mistakes**

Avoid:

-   Selecting exercises for novelty rather than purpose

-   Repeating the same movement family every class

-   Progressing load before control is demonstrated

-   Leaving out a known regression for a challenging exercise

-   Building a sequence with no discernible through-line

**Selection Checklist**

> **✓** Every exercise supports today\'s stated objective
>
> **✓** A regression and progression exist for the primary movements
>
> **✓** The movement families are balanced across the week
>
> **✓** The sequence has a clear beginning, middle, and peak

**SECTION 07**

**TEMPO, BREATH & INTENSITY**

*The SPACE Equivalent of Volume and Load*

SPACE classes rarely rely on heavy external load. Instead, intensity is built through tempo, breath, range of motion, and time under tension.

Understanding how to manipulate these variables is what separates purposeful programming from simply doing more.

**Tempo**

Tempo controls how much time a muscle spends working within a single repetition. Slower tempo increases control demand and time under tension. Faster tempo, applied intentionally, builds coordination and cardiovascular demand --- most relevant in GLIDE.

Tempo should always be a deliberate choice, cued clearly, and matched to the objective of the exercise --- never left to drift.

**Breath as an Intensity Tool**

Breath is not just a cueing device --- it is a programming variable.

Exhaling fully on the working phase of a movement increases core engagement and control. Extending the exhale during a hold increases time under tension without adding external resistance.

**Range of Motion**

A smaller, precisely controlled range often builds more strength and stability than a larger, uncontrolled one. Range should expand only as control allows --- not as a default marker of progress.

**Resistance & Equipment**

Where equipment is used --- springs, gliders, light weights, bands --- resistance should be layered onto an already-controlled pattern, never used to compensate for a pattern that isn\'t yet owned.

**The Four Intensity Levers**

  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------
  **Tempo**            Speed of the repetition

  **Range**            How much of the available motion is used

  **Resistance**       Springs, gliders, bands, or bodyweight load

  **Density**          Rest between sets or flow between exercises
  -------------------- --------------------------------------------------

Only one or two levers should shift meaningfully within a single class. Changing all four at once removes an instructor\'s ability to isolate what is actually driving the challenge.

**Key Takeaways**

-   Intensity is built through tempo, breath, range, and resistance --- not just \"more.\"

-   Breath is an active programming variable, not background noise.

-   Range of motion expands only as control allows.

-   Adjust one or two intensity levers per class, not all four.

**SECTION 08**

**PROGRAMMING THE MEMBER EXPERIENCE**

*Meeting Members Where They Are*

A class can be technically well-built and still fail a member if it does not meet them at their current level.

Programming the member experience means designing classes --- and modifications within them --- that keep every member, regardless of starting point, moving safely toward the same objective.

**The Three Member Stages**

  -------------------- -----------------------------------------------------------------
  **New**              Learning breath, center, and foundational alignment

  **Developing**       Owns the fundamentals; building control under increasing demand

  **Established**      Ready for full range, added resistance, and dynamic sequencing
  -------------------- -----------------------------------------------------------------

Every class should offer a clear path for all three stages simultaneously --- this is what allows SPACE to run mixed-level classes without diluting the experience for anyone.

**Modifications Are a First-Class Citizen**

A modification should never be announced as an afterthought or a lesser option. It should be cued with the same confidence as the primary version, framed around what it builds rather than what it lacks.

**Special Populations**

Instructors should be prepared with informed modifications for common circumstances, including prenatal and postpartum members, those returning from injury, and members managing chronic conditions such as low back pain or joint sensitivity. When in doubt, regress toward control rather than push toward range.

**Onboarding a New Member**

A new member\'s first few classes should prioritize breath, center, and alignment over keeping pace with the room. Early wins should come from feeling the correct muscles work --- not from finishing every repetition the rest of the class completes.

**Key Takeaways**

-   Every class should serve new, developing, and established members at once.

-   Modifications are taught with the same authority as the primary movement.

-   New members are onboarded around control, not pace.

-   When uncertain, regress toward control rather than push toward range.

**SECTION 09**

**GROUND**

*Where Strength Begins*

GROUND is the foundational training system of SPACE.

It is designed to build the center-first strength, control, and body awareness that every other format depends on --- using mat-based work, bodyweight, and light props.

The objective is not simply to \"do Pilates.\" The objective is to build a member who owns their center, understands their breath, and moves with precision before they ever add speed or resistance.

**The Philosophy**

GROUND is built on one simple idea:

Master the foundation. Everything else gets easier.

Members should not chase advanced sequencing before they own the fundamentals. Instead, they continually deepen control and center connection --- the qualities every other SPACE format is built on top of.

**Primary Training Objectives**

Every GROUND class should prioritize one or more of the following:

-   Breath mechanics and powerhouse activation

-   Spinal articulation and control

-   Postural alignment

-   Core stability (anti-extension, anti-rotation)

-   Foundational strength endurance

-   Body awareness and precision

**The GROUND Member**

A GROUND-trained member should be able to:

-   Find and hold a neutral spine under increasing demand

-   Breathe through effort instead of holding tension

-   Move a single joint without recruiting the whole body

-   Feel the difference between working the target muscle and compensating around it

-   Carry postural awareness outside the studio

Precision --- not intensity --- is the goal.

**Class Blueprint**

Most GROUND classes follow:

Center & Breathe → Spinal Warm-Up → Foundational Core Series → Full-Body Control Sequence → Targeted Strength → Release

The emphasis shifts week to week. The structure remains consistent.

**Signature Elements**

GROUND draws heavily from the classical mat repertoire: the hundred, roll-up, single and double leg stretch, spine stretch, saw, and side-lying series --- layered with small props such as the magic circle, mini ball, and light hand weights to add resistance without sacrificing control.

**Common Mistakes**

Avoid:

-   Rushing repetitions to fit more exercises into the class

-   Adding resistance before breath and center are established

-   Treating GROUND as a \"warm-up\" format rather than a complete class

-   Skipping the release sequence to save time

**Programming Checklist**

> **✓** Breath and center are established before load increases
>
> **✓** Spinal articulation appears in every class
>
> **✓** Core work balances flexion, extension, and rotation
>
> **✓** Every class ends with deliberate release

**The GROUND Standard**

GROUND should produce members who move with precision.

Centered enough to stabilize. Controlled enough to progress safely. Aware enough to carry it into daily life.

Every class should move a member one step closer to owning their foundation.

Good for: first-timers, anyone returning after time away, and anyone who wants the foundation done properly.

**SECTION 10**

**GLIDE**

*Full-Body Flow in Motion*

GLIDE is the signature flow system of SPACE.

It is designed to build cardiovascular capacity, muscular endurance, and full-body coordination through low-impact, high-intensity sliding and gliding sequences layered on top of a Pilates foundation.

The objective is not to abandon control for the sake of intensity. The objective is to prove that control and intensity can coexist --- that a member can move quickly, breathe hard, and still stay centered.

**The Philosophy**

GLIDE is built on one simple idea:

Move with purpose. Never lose your center.

Every glide, slide, and transition should be powered from a stable center --- not momentum. Strength supports flow. Flow supports endurance. Endurance supports confidence in motion.

**Primary Training Objectives**

Every GLIDE class should prioritize one or more of the following:

-   Cardiovascular endurance

-   Full-body coordination

-   Muscular stamina under continuous tension

-   Dynamic core stability

-   Fluid, connected transitions

-   Low-impact power development

**The GLIDE Member**

A GLIDE-trained member should be able to:

-   Maintain center through continuous, dynamic movement

-   Sustain elevated effort while controlling their breath

-   Transition between exercises without resetting from scratch

-   Produce power through the legs and core simultaneously

-   Recover quickly between higher-intensity intervals

**Class Blueprint**

Most GLIDE classes follow:

Center & Breathe → Dynamic Warm-Up → Foundational Glide Series → Flow Intervals → Full Combination → Release

Unlike GROUND, GLIDE intentionally elevates heart rate through sustained, connected movement --- but every interval still opens and closes with a center check.

**Signature Elements**

GLIDE is built on the Heroboard --- a sliding platform that recreates the resistance and control of reformer-style work. The carriage moves, the member\'s body stabilizes, and everything gets harder than it looks. Because the surface itself demands stability, GLIDE builds core control almost incidentally --- there is no shortcut through the slide without losing center.

It is not a reformer, and it is never described as one. \"Reformer-style\" only, every time. Members who have taken a reformer class will know the difference within thirty seconds, and closing that gap in language matters more than it seems.

**Conditioning Philosophy**

Conditioning in GLIDE should always support control, not replace it.

Every interval has a defined objective --- sustained power, muscular endurance, or coordination under fatigue. Intensity should never be programmed simply to leave members breathless with no purpose behind it.

**Common Mistakes**

Avoid:

-   Letting tempo outrun control

-   Programming maximal intensity every class

-   Skipping the center check between intervals

-   Treating GLIDE as \"cardio only\" and neglecting strength development

**Programming Checklist**

> **✓** Every interval has a stated purpose beyond \"go hard\"
>
> **✓** Center is re-established between combinations
>
> **✓** Intensity is varied intentionally across the week
>
> **✓** The class builds toward a clear, satisfying final combination

**The GLIDE Standard**

GLIDE should produce members who are strong in motion.

Fluid enough to connect. Powerful enough to sustain. Controlled enough to never lose their center, no matter how fast they\'re moving.

Good for: anyone who has done Pilates before, anyone curious about reformer-style work, and anyone who wants to be humbled by a sliding board.

**SECTION 11**

**FORM**

*Strength, Programmed Properly*

FORM is the strength system of SPACE --- Pilates-informed strength work built with dumbbells, rings, bands, and bodyweight, sequenced by a coach with fourteen years in strength and conditioning.

Slower than a bootcamp. Heavier than a mat class. Built to make a member durable.

Strength is not the opposite of ease. It is what makes ease possible. FORM exists to build a body that carries a member --- not one that performs for anyone.

**The Philosophy**

FORM is built on one simple idea:

Build the capacity that makes the rest of life take less out of you.

Members should leave FORM having worked to a genuine, controlled fatigue --- while never losing the alignment and breath principles that define every SPACE format.

**Primary Training Objectives**

Every FORM class should prioritize one or more of the following:

-   Muscular endurance to genuine fatigue

-   Targeted strength development (arms, glutes, back, core)

-   Postural strength and shoulder stability

-   Progressive external resistance

-   Durable, everyday-carryover strength

**The FORM Member**

A FORM-trained member should be able to:

-   Isolate and fatigue a specific muscle group with control

-   Maintain alignment through the final, hardest repetitions

-   Progress resistance without sacrificing range or form

-   Feel the difference in what their body can carry, day to day

**Class Blueprint**

Most FORM classes follow:

Center & Breathe → Warm-Up → Foundational Core Series → Targeted Strength Circuit → Isolation Finisher → Release

FORM spends more time than GROUND or GLIDE in targeted, muscle-group-specific work --- but always opens from the same center-first foundation.

**Signature Elements**

FORM layers light hand weights, resistance bands, and ankle weights onto classical Pilates patterns --- arm circles, glute bridges, side-lying leg series, and seated rowing patterns --- to create genuine muscular fatigue at low joint load.

**Strength Philosophy**

Resistance should progress across weeks, not within a single set at the expense of form. Isolation work should target real muscular imbalances, not simply chase fatigue for its own sake.

**Common Mistakes**

Avoid:

-   Adding resistance before the bodyweight version is controlled

-   Over-programming one muscle group and neglecting its opposing pattern

-   Sacrificing range of motion to complete more repetitions

-   Framing FORM around how a member looks rather than what they can now carry

**Programming Checklist**

> **✓** Resistance matches the member\'s demonstrated control
>
> **✓** Opposing muscle groups are balanced across the class
>
> **✓** The final set reaches genuine, controlled fatigue
>
> **✓** Alignment holds through the last repetition, not just the first

**The FORM Standard**

FORM should produce members who are functionally stronger --- strong through full range, aligned even at fatigue, and carrying more capacity into daily life.

Good for: anyone who wants to build capacity, anyone crossing over from the gym, and anyone who has been told to \"get stronger\" without being told how.

SECTIONS 12--14

**SECTION 12**

**THE SPACE QUALITY STANDARD**

*What Every Member Should Feel*

Programming is only half of the SPACE experience. The other half is delivery --- the room, the cueing, and the consistency members can count on regardless of which instructor is teaching.

**Cueing Standards**

Every instructor cues in the same order, regardless of format: breath, then center, then the working muscle, then range or tempo. This sequence should feel automatic --- a member should be able to close their eyes and know exactly where in the cueing arc the class is.

**The Room**

Temperature, lighting, and music are part of the class, not incidental to it. GROUND favors a calmer, warmer room and softer lighting. GLIDE favors more energetic music and a slightly cooler room to support sustained effort. FORM sits between the two --- energized but controlled.

**Consistency Across Instructors**

A member should be able to attend a GROUND class from any instructor and recognize the same standards, the same cueing language, and the same class arc. Individual coaching style is welcome. Deviation from the blueprint is not.

**The First Five Minutes**

A member decides whether they trust the room within the first five minutes of class. Instructors should greet members by name where possible, confirm any injuries or modifications needed that day, and set the intention for the class before the warm-up begins.

**Key Takeaways**

-   Cueing follows the same order --- breath, center, muscle, range --- in every format.

-   The room\'s temperature, lighting, and music are programmed, not incidental.

-   Consistency across instructors matters more than individual flair.

-   Trust is built or lost in the first five minutes of class.

**SECTION 13**

**THE INSTRUCTOR PROGRAMMING PLAYBOOK**

*From Concept to Class*

This section is the working checklist instructors use when building or auditing a class before it\'s taught.

**Before You Program**

> **✓** What is this class\'s single stated objective?
>
> **✓** Which format, and which weekly identity, does this class serve?
>
> **✓** What movement families were used in the last two classes this week?
>
> **✓** What level of member is expected in the room?

**While You Program**

> **✓** Does the sequence follow the SPACE Class Blueprint (Center, Warm, Foundation, Develop, Support, Express, Release)?
>
> **✓** Is there a known regression and progression for every primary exercise?
>
> **✓** Are the movement patterns balanced (flexion/extension, push/pull, left/right)?
>
> **✓** Have no more than one or two intensity levers (tempo, range, resistance, density) shifted meaningfully?

**Before You Teach**

> **✓** Can the objective be explained to a member in one sentence?
>
> **✓** Is the class the right length for its format?
>
> **✓** Does the class end with deliberate release?
>
> **✓** Would this class meet the SPACE Standard: build, teach, and prepare the member?

**After You Teach**

The best programming improves through feedback. Instructors should note which cues landed, where members lost center, and which regressions were used most --- and bring those notes into the next round of programming.
