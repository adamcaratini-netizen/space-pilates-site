For Pilates, wear fitted, stretchy clothes you can move in freely, plus a pair of grippy socks. Skip anything loose, stiff or with hard embellishments, and tie back long hair. Bring a water bottle, and a small towel if you like.

## Key takeaways

- Fitted, breathable clothing lets you move freely and lets your instructor see your alignment.
- Grippy socks give you traction and hygiene on the mat. Many studios require them.
- Tie long hair low or in a braid so it stays comfortable when you lie on your back.
- Bring a water bottle and an optional sweat towel. Most studios provide mats and props.
- Leave jewelry, zippers, buckles and very loose layers at home.

## Why what you wear matters

Pilates is a practice of precision. You roll, twist, lie on your back and stomach, and lift your legs overhead. Clothing that shifts, bunches or digs in becomes a distraction, and it pulls your attention away from breath and control.

Fitted clothing also helps your instructor. When they can see the line of your spine, hips and knees, they can offer clearer cues and better modifications.

## Clothing for mat Pilates

### Tops

Choose a fitted top or tank that stays in place when you are upside down. A loose T-shirt slides toward your face when your legs are overhead.

A supportive sports bra is a good idea. Choose one without metal clasps or thick padding at the back, since you spend time lying on your spine.

### Bottoms

Leggings, fitted capris or close-fitting shorts are all good choices. Look for:

- **Stretch**, so nothing restricts your hips or knees.
- **Opacity**, so you feel comfortable in deep bends and wide-legged positions.
- **A secure waistband** that stays put when you roll and curl.

### Fabric

Breathable, moisture-wicking fabrics are the most comfortable. Heavy cotton can feel damp and clingy by the end of class.

## Grippy socks and why they matter

Grippy socks have small rubber dots or pads on the sole. They are one of the most useful things you can bring to a Pilates class, and many studios require them.

Here is why:

- **Traction.** Grips keep your feet from sliding on the mat or floor during standing work, lunges and planks, so you can focus on control.
- **Hygiene.** Socks keep shared mats and floors cleaner for everyone.
- **Warmth and comfort.** Many people simply prefer something on their feet.

Regular socks slip. Bare feet are allowed in some studios but not others, so check before you go. At Space Pilates + Wellness Society, grippy socks are required in every class, and they are sold at the studio if you need a pair.

## Hair, jewelry and small details

### Hair

Tie long hair back in a way that is comfortable when you lie on your back. A high ponytail or a large clip can press into your head during supine exercises. A low ponytail, a braid or a low bun usually works best. Bring an extra hair tie.

### Jewelry

Take off rings, bracelets, watches with hard edges and dangling earrings. They can catch on clothing or props, press into your skin and get in the way when you grip a ring or a weight.

## What to bring

Most studios keep things simple. Here is what to pack:

1. **Grippy socks**, if your studio requires them.
2. **A water bottle.** Sip between exercises as needed.
3. **A small sweat towel**, optional but nice, especially for sculpt and strength classes.
4. **A layer for after class**, if you tend to feel chilly once you cool down.

Mats and props such as rings, bands and weights are usually provided. If you are unsure, ask the studio before your first visit. Plan to arrive about 10 minutes early so you can settle in and let the instructor know about any injuries or concerns. For more first-class tips, read [Pilates for beginners](/learn/pilates-for-beginners).

## What not to wear

- **Loose, baggy tops** that slide up when you are inverted.
- **Stiff fabrics** like denim or heavy canvas.
- **Zippers, buckles, snaps and rivets**, which press into your back and can damage mats.
- **Thick back clasps or bulky hair accessories** that dig in when you lie down.
- **Regular socks** without grips.

## Adjusting for sculpt, strength and yoga

The same basics apply across class formats, with a few small adjustments.

### Sculpt

[Sculpt](/classes/sculpt) classes are rhythm-driven and set to music, with dumbbells, bands and bodyweight. Expect to get warm. Moisture-wicking fabric and a sweat towel are especially helpful, and grippy socks keep you steady through faster transitions.

### Strength

[Strength](/classes/strength) classes are slower and heavier, with dumbbells, kettlebells and resistance bands. Fitted clothing lets your coach see your positions during squats, hinges and presses. Grippy socks give you a stable base for lifting. Avoid long, loose sleeves that get in the way of a kettlebell or dumbbell.

### Yoga

For restorative [yoga](/classes/yoga), comfort comes first. You may hold supported positions for several minutes, so soft, stretchy layers work well. Bring a light layer, since the body tends to cool down during slower, quieter practice. Follow your studio's footwear guidance.

## Questions

### Can I wear regular socks to Pilates?

Regular socks tend to slip on mats and floors, which makes standing and plank work harder to control. Grippy socks are the better choice, and many studios require them.

### Should I wear shoes to a Pilates class?

Mat Pilates is usually done in grippy socks or bare feet, not shoes. Check your studio's policy before your first class.

### Is it okay to wear loose clothes to Pilates?

Very loose clothes can shift during rolling and inverted exercises and make it harder for your instructor to see your alignment. Fitted, stretchy clothes are more comfortable and practical.

### What should I bring to my first Pilates class?

Grippy socks, a water bottle and an optional sweat towel. Most studios provide mats and props, and it helps to arrive about 10 minutes early.
